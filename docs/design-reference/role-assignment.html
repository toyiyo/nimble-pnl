Putting people in a role

A role is a room. You should be able to walk in from either door — from the person (what is José?) or from the role (who is in Operations lead?). Today both doors are painted on: the role picker only offers built-ins, and the member count on a role card is dead text. These three moves open them.

Move 1 — the chip is the control

Today the role is decoration sitting beside a ⋯ that hides a dropdown menu that contains a select. Three layers of chrome to reach a picker, and the picker only ever lists the closed Role union. Here the chip itself is the button, and the list is every role this restaurant actually has.

Team members

Click anyone's role to move them.

Move 2 — the member count is a door

“0 people” is exactly where you looked when you wanted to apply the role you had just built, and it did nothing. It becomes the primary action on an unused role: a face pile when the role has members, a terracotta Assign people when it doesn't.

Your roles

Click a role's name to change its areas — or its people count to change who's in it.

Who's in this role

Everyone here reaches the same areas. Changing the areas changes it for all of them the next time they load a page.

The areas matrix — unchanged from the approved roles-and-areas.html prototype. This tab is only reframed as a sibling of People, so a role reads as “areas + people” rather than “areas, and nowhere to put anyone.”

Move 3 — the employee record stops lying

A role lives on the account, not on the employee row — which is why looking for it on an employee found nothing. Rather than bolt a fake role field onto the employee dialog, the dialog names the real thing: an App access row that shows the linked account's role and links to it. And the invite it sends stops hard-coding staff.
